## Title
What a drone actually adds to a wedding video (and what it doesn't)

## Standfirst
Two shots. That's what the drone earns you. Everything else is the ceremony doing the real work.

## Body

Every couple asks about the drone. Here is my honest answer: the drone earns you about two shots in a wedding film.

The first is the establishing shot, the venue from above, wide and slow, maybe fifteen seconds at the open. The second is a slow pullback at golden hour, if the light cooperates. Those two shots cost me a Part 107 license, airspace checks, a weather window, and a second pair of hands on the ground making sure nobody walks into the flight line.

What it does not do: it does not fix boring coverage. I have watched drone pilots deliver ten minutes of sky when the ceremony itself was shot flat. The vows are what your family watches in ten years. Nobody rewinds the wedding film for a nice view of the parking lot.

And the venue matters. Half the places I shoot in Montgomery County sit inside controlled airspace or have hard no-fly rules. I check before the day, every time, and if the answer is no, the film does not suffer. A high window or a slow ground move gives you the same feeling for less money.

Hire the drone when the place deserves it. Do not hire it because the package sounded cool.
